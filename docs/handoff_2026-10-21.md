## Paper-inspired directions discussed

These are research directions, not newly approved or executed work.

### 1. ReMoSense-style motion-aware architecture

The ReVA paper proposes a motion module with two kinds of features: global inter-frame correspondences to represent camera/viewpoint movement, and recurrent object-motion tokens to preserve object information across frames. In plain terms, it tries to tell “the camera moved” apart from “the object moved,” while carrying object identity through the clip. This is the most directly domain-matched paper direction for ReVA's temporal and viewpoint questions. It is an architecture change and requires training; it is not equivalent to increasing LoRA epochs. The paper reports training on ReVA and a two-80-GB-A100 setup, so its result is not a low-cost plug-in estimate. Its paper results should not be compared to our score without confirming identical split and evaluation protocol. [ReVA / ReMoSense paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35507v1)

### 2. BIMBA-style temporal token selection

BIMBA uses a selective-scan state-space module to compress a large sequence of video tokens while retaining informative long-range content. The intended benefit is handling long or redundant video more efficiently than simply taking sparse frames or pooling them. ReVA clips are short and the current Stage A recipe uses 16 sampled frames, so BIMBA is a weaker fit unless the experiment changes to many more frames or longer contexts. It is also a substantial architecture/model change, not a small inference wrapper. [BIMBA CVPR paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Islam_BIMBA_Selective-Scan_Compression_for_Long-Range_Video_Question_Answering_CVPR_2025_paper.html)

### 3. Task-specific reinforcement fine-tuning (GRPO / RFT)

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) samples several candidate outputs for the same example and reinforces candidates with better verifiable rewards. VideoChat-R1 and Video-R1 motivate task-specific spatiotemporal rewards instead of rewarding only the final answer. For example, a structured temporal output could contain both an option and an interval; the reward could include exact answer correctness and interval overlap. A geometric arm could score relation correctness and verified box overlap. The current competition output is one letter, so these extra rewards need a separate structured training target and clean labels; they cannot be inferred from the letter. These papers show a direction, not a guaranteed ReVA gain. [VideoChat-R1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06958), [Video-R1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21776)

### 4. Temporal label review and difficulty curriculum

VideoTG-R1 identifies incomplete event intervals and hard-to-ground samples before or during training, then uses a curriculum so the model learns from clearer examples before harder ones. This matters for ReVA because the project's audit of 100 candidate temporal training examples found 10 directly usable, 57 conditional on repair/re-review, and 33 to exclude. Until that data gate improves, applying a temporal reward to questionable boundaries could train toward the wrong target. [VideoTG-R1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23397); see the local [temporal data audit](../results/temporal_training_data_audit/phase1_report.md).

### 5. GeoVLM-R1 task-aware reward design

GeoVLM-R1 uses task-specific rewards for remote-sensing image tasks such as VQA, grounding, detection, and change descriptions. Its transferable idea is matching the reward to the target skill. The paper is image-focused, however; it does not demonstrate continuous video tracking or timestamp grounding on ReVA. It should be treated as a reward-design reference, not a ReVA checkpoint or proven video solution. [GeoVLM-R1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25026); local [transfer assessment](research/geovlm_r1_transfer_assessment.md) and [RL recipe notes](research/reva_rl_recipe_sources.md).

## Relevant experiments already recorded in the repository

The experiment ledger is the source of record for denominators, caveats, and artifact paths: [experiment ledger](experiment_ledger.md).

- **Frozen Stage A reference:** Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct LoRA r16, 16 legacy frames, two offsets and two option rotations. `valQ` 498/595 (83.70%); `valT` 885/1,059 (83.57%); official validation 1,757/2,000 (87.85%).
- **Endpoint / adaptive sampling:** endpoint sampling reached 87.75% on official validation. Adaptive sampling reached 87.25%; Temporal Grounding lost six answers on the 320-question official-validation slice. Adaptive sampling was not promoted.
- **Question-aware coarse-to-fine temporal sampling:** on Temporal Grounding, T1 was −2 net correct on `valQ` and −1 on `valT`; timestamp-context T2 was −4 and −8. Neither passed its predeclared gate. The 24-question reviewed-window subset was only a diagnostic, not split-wide evidence. See [Phase 2 report](../runs/phase2_temporal_coarsefine/phase2_report.md).
- **Detector / mask / crop evidence:** GroundingDINO matched the baseline on the selected 16-question-per-split pilot. SAM pilots showed some local fixes but did not establish broad gains. The explicit geometry comparison had no answer changes on the selected 30 questions per split. Retrieved-crop E2 made no fixes against video-only E0 on its selected 12-question diagnostic cohort.
- **Continued visual adaptation:** recorded A1–A4 experiments did not pass the declared promotion gate; some variants regressed on `valT`. This is evidence against assuming that more adapter training or trainable vision layers automatically improve results.
- **Phase 8 backbone comparison:** local Qwen3.8 records include complete `valQ` 506/595 (85.04%) and only partial `valT`; the recorded H100 path used a PEFT adapter. The current user-provided 4,000-pair metrics must be connected to their exact output and manifest before merging them into the formal experiment ledger.

These findings support avoiding another uncontrolled “train longer” run. They do not prove that every architecture or reward-learning approach will fail.
